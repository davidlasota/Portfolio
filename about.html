About Me

The Analyst
Behind the Work

A detail-oriented Business Analyst based in Chicago, IL — focused on driving measurable outcomes in healthcare and insurance through data-driven strategy and process excellence.

Who I Am

I'm David LaSota, a Chicago-based Business Analyst with a passion for turning organizational complexity into clear, actionable strategy. My work sits at the intersection of data, process design, and stakeholder communication — translating what's broken into blueprints for what works.


I'm drawn to the healthcare and insurance industries because the stakes are real. A faster referral process means a patient gets care sooner. A cleaner claims intake workflow means fewer denied claims and more members served. That human dimension makes every project matter.

My Approach

I believe that the best requirements documentation starts with deep listening. Before writing a single user story, I invest heavily in stakeholder discovery — understanding not just what people say they need, but the underlying workflows and pain points driving that need.


I pair structured analysis with visual storytelling: process diagrams, dashboards, and gap analyses that communicate to both the C-suite and the front-line team. My goal is always clarity — documents and deliverables that leave no room for misinterpretation at implementation time.

Healthcare Focus

I understand the operational realities of health systems: revenue cycle management, EHR integration challenges, HIPAA compliance requirements, and the pressure to improve patient experience while controlling costs. My projects are grounded in Illinois-specific context — including ICD-10 standards, Illinois Medicaid (HFS), and the workflows of Chicago-area health systems.

Insurance Focus

On the insurance side, I'm versed in claims adjudication fundamentals, EDI transaction sets (837/835), denial management workflows, and member eligibility verification. I understand the HCSC/BCBS IL ecosystem and the operational pressures facing regional insurers — from intake error rates to regulatory reporting demands.
